A clean setup, a sensible stop, and then a 40-pip candle out of nowhere. Most traders learn about the economic calendar this way: by getting stopped out by a release they didn't know was coming. Trading around news is not about predicting the number. It is about knowing *when* the market can jump and deciding *before* it happens what you will do.

## What an economic calendar shows

An economic calendar lists scheduled releases and events: the time, the currency affected, an impact rating (usually low, medium or high) and, for data, the forecast and the previous value. The market reacts to the **surprise**, the gap between the actual number and what was expected, not to the number itself. A strong jobs report that was already expected can move the price less than a slightly weak one that wasn't.

## The releases that move the market most

For the major forex pairs and gold, these are the events most traders mark first:

| Event | Usually | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| US Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP) | First Friday of the month, 08:30 New York | Jobs data drives expectations for Fed rates; often the biggest scheduled move of the month in USD pairs and gold |
| US CPI (inflation) | Monthly, 08:30 New York | Inflation surprises reprice rate expectations fast |
| FOMC rate decision | 8 scheduled meetings a year, statement 14:00 New York, press conference 14:30 | The rate decision, the statement and the press conference can each move the market |
| ECB, BoE, BoJ and other rate decisions | Fixed calendar each year | Move their own currencies and the crosses |
| GDP, retail sales, PMIs | Monthly or quarterly | Usually smaller moves, bigger when the surprise is large |

Speeches by central bank heads can move the market too, but they are harder to time: the market reacts to a phrase, not a number.

## What happens at the release

In the seconds around a high-impact release, the market changes character:

- **Spreads widen.** Liquidity providers pull back just before the number. A pair that normally trades with a spread under one pip can show several pips for a short time.
- **Price gaps.** The first move can skip price levels entirely. A stop at a level the price jumped over is filled at the next available price, which can be well beyond it.
- **Whipsaw.** The first spike often reverses within minutes, sometimes in both directions, before the real move starts.

All of this means a −1R stop can turn into a −1.5R or −2R loss, and a tight stop placed just before the release has a good chance of being hit by noise.

## Three ways to handle news

There is no single right approach. What matters is that you choose one before the session and stick to it.

### 1. Stay out

Close or avoid new positions for a window around high-impact releases on the currencies you trade, for example 15 minutes before to 15 minutes after. This is the simplest rule and the one most traders who trade technical setups end up with.

### 2. Trade after the dust settles

Let the first spike happen, wait for spreads to normalise and for price to show a direction, then trade your normal setup in the new environment. Many of the cleanest trends of the week start 15 to 30 minutes after a major release.

### 3. Trade the news on purpose

Some traders build strategies around the release itself. It can work, but it needs fast execution, a broker that fills well in fast markets, smaller size to account for slippage, and a tested plan. Don't do it by accident because a trade was still open.

> **Prop firm traders:** some firms restrict trading around high-impact news on funded accounts, or treat profits from it differently. The rules differ between firms and change over time; check yours before a news week.

## A pre-session news routine

1. **Check the calendar before the session**, filtered to the currencies you trade. Note every high-impact event and its time in your time zone.
2. **Decide the plan for each one**: stay out, wait, or trade it. Write it down.
3. **Check open positions** an hour before a release. Decide whether to close, tighten, reduce or hold, while you are calm.
4. **Don't place tight new stops** in the minutes before a high-impact release.
5. **Mark news trades in your journal** so you can measure them later.

## Measure what news costs you

The question "should I trade the news?" has a factual answer in your own trades. Tag or note every trade that was open during a high-impact release, then compare after 30 or more of them:

- Win rate and average result of news trades vs all other trades
- How many losses went beyond −1R because of slippage
- Whether your results on high-impact days are better or worse than on quiet days

Many traders find that a small number of news trades accounts for a large share of their worst losses. Others find the opposite. Either way, you decide on data instead of memory.

## Key takeaways

- The market moves on the surprise versus the forecast, not on the number itself.
- NFP, CPI and central bank decisions move forex and gold the most; know their times in your time zone.
- Around releases spreads widen, prices gap and stops can fill well beyond their level.
- Pick one approach before the session: stay out, wait for the dust to settle, or trade the news on purpose with a tested plan.
- Mark news trades in your journal and let the numbers decide.
